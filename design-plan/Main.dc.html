Website plan · September 2026
Heaven's Harvest Pantry
A neighborhood granola shop that fits in a text message.
What we are building

A website where neighbors in Vistancia and Anthem can see this week's granola, order in under a minute from their phone, choose a bag or a mason jar, pick delivery or a drop-off, and pay by card or Apple Pay.

Behind it sits a private owner's page. Every order, today's delivery run, who owes a jar back, what to bake this week. No spreadsheets, no DMs to chase.

The promise to a customer
01Scan the jar, see the flavors, order in three taps.
02Bring the mason jar back and take $2 off.
03Every dollar earns a point. 100 points is a free bag.
04A text when it's on the way, and when it's on the porch.
The pages a visitor sees
Home

Her story in two sentences, this week's flavors, one button: Order. Big photography, warm paper, the leaf motif from the logo.

The Menu

Every flavor with a photo, ingredients and allergens, and a jar-or-bag switch. Sold-out flavors show it. Prices change from her phone.

Checkout

One screen. Jar or bag, "returning a jar?", delivery or drop-off with the next date, then Apple Pay, Google Pay or card. No account needed.

Rewards

Sign in with a phone number and a code, no password. See points, jars returned, and the next reward. Birthday bag on the house.

Reviews

Star ratings and a few words from real customers, asked for by text two days after delivery. She approves each one before it shows.

Our Story & Where We Deliver

Why she bakes, what goes in, the Vistancia and Anthem delivery days, and the drop-off spots on a simple map.

The Verse Archive

Every weekly verse card she has sent out, kept in one place, so someone can find the one from the week they needed it.

Blessing Bags

Buy a bag for a neighbor in a hard season. Suggest a name, or let her choose. Churches can request a batch at once.

Faith, woven in rather than bolted on
The gospel shows up as hospitality, not as decoration.

Scripture belongs where it means something on a granola site: at the table, in the giving, and in the work of her hands. Six verses carry the page. Nothing is stacked up as ornament, and nothing is used as a sales tactic.

She can change every verse herself, week by week, without calling anyone.

How it is built, in plain terms
Lives on Brandon's Vercel account under her own domain. Public, fast, backed up, not sitting on a laptop.
Money moves through Stripe straight into her bank. No monthly fee, about 2.9% plus 30¢ per order. Apple Pay and Google Pay come with it. She gets her own login to see payouts.
Orders, customers, jars and reviews live in a hosted database that is free at her size and keeps daily backups.
Texts and emails for order confirmations, "on the way", and the review ask. About a penny a text.
Built to hand over. She never needs a developer to change a price, add a flavor, or pause deliveries for a week.

Arizona home kitchen rules · worth knowing

Granola is a shelf-stable baked good, which Arizona's cottage food program allows to be sold online and delivered. Registration with the state health department is free: a short online food-safety course and a form.

Labels need the business name and address, the ingredients, allergens, and a "made in a home kitchen" line. The site will print matching labels from the same flavor list, so the label and the website never disagree.

There is no cap on how much she can sell. If she ever wants to ship out of state, that is a different license, and the site is ready for it.
