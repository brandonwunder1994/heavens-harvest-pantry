# Social Media — the plan

Heaven's Harvest Pantry · Instagram · written 2026-09-02

Two things live in this document. The first is a **content system**: 100 finished
posts and the rhythm that turns them into a year of Instagram. The second is a
**build plan**: how a Social Media tab gets added to her owner's page, and how her
Instagram account gets connected so posts can be scheduled and published without
her opening the app.

Nothing here is built yet. This is the thing to approve or change.

---

## 1. What we are actually trying to do

Lauren bakes on Monday and delivers Tuesday and Friday. The list closes Sunday at
nine. Everything about the business is a weekly loop, and Instagram has to sit
inside that loop rather than beside it.

So the target is not "grow the account." The target is:

1. **A stranger in Peoria stops scrolling** — because the post is funny, or
   beautiful, or true, or all three.
2. **They tap the bio link** and land on the shop.
3. **They order before Sunday at nine.**

Everything in this plan is judged against those three steps. A post that gets
four hundred likes from people in Ohio has failed. A post that gets nineteen
likes and two orders from Vistancia has worked.

The second job, quieter and slower: the account is the front door for the
**blessing bags** and the **verse card**. Those are the two things nobody else in
the granola business has, and they are the reason someone follows an account
about oats.

---

## 2. What actually works on Instagram right now

This is the research, compressed. Every one of the 100 posts is built on it.

**Reach in 2026 is driven by three signals**, in this order: watch time (did they
finish it), sends (did they DM it to someone), and saves. Likes are close to
decorative. This changes what a post should be built to do — every one of the 100
has a stated `goal` of watch, saves, shares, comments or clicks, and the post is
shaped around that one goal rather than trying to do all five.

**The first line does all the work.** Instagram measures whether people swipe
away early. The hook has to be specific enough to be believed and narrow enough
to feel personally addressed. "Three things nutritionists actually eat for
breakfast" beats "healthy breakfast ideas" every time. So the hooks in this
library are things like *"This jar has been to eleven houses"* and *"The pickup
line is twenty-two minutes long. Ask me how I know"* — never *"Try our delicious
granola."*

**Specificity is the whole game.** Numbers, street names, times of day, the real
temperature, the real number of trays. Generic food-brand language is invisible;
a woman saying "Monday, 5:41 a.m., Peoria is still dark" is not.

**Carousels earn swipes, which are dwell time.** Anything that is a list, a
build, a ranking, or a how-to should be a carousel with the payoff on the last
slide. 17 of the 100 are carousels for exactly this reason.

**Short loops beat long videos.** A six-second reel that loops cleanly gets a
completion rate a thirty-second one cannot touch. Sound on. Crunch, honey
pouring, a jar lid popping — food audio is the cheapest retention there is.

**Save-bait must be explicit.** "Save this for the week you forget you have it"
outperforms the same post without the line. 25 of the 100 are built for saves.

**Send-bait must name a person.** Not "share this" but "you know exactly who in
your family this is. Send it to them." Sends are the single most weighted signal
and this is how you get them.

**Hashtags are nearly dead but not free-dead.** Three to five, mostly local. Every
post here carries five: a couple of local (`#peoriaaz`, `#vistancia`,
`#anthemaz`) and a couple of niche. They are for discovery in the local map, not
for reach.

**Faith content works when it costs the writer something.** Verse-plus-pretty-
photo is scrolled past. A verse taped above the counter, with an admission that
some weeks it is a job description rather than an encouragement, is not.

**The one rule that overrides all of the above:** nothing invented. No fake
reviews, no invented backstory, no number she has not counted. 23 of the 100
posts are marked *needs a real detail* — they are frames with her fact missing,
and the page will not let her forget it.

## 3. The content system

### The twelve pillars

| Pillar | Count | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **funny** | 12 | Stops the scroll and gets sent to one person |
| **faith** | 11 | The reason someone follows a granola account |
| **useful** | 11 | Saves. Recipes, storage, lunchboxes, quantities |
| **bake** | 9 | Process footage. Proves the price is real |
| **local** | 9 | Converts neighbors. The one thing a big brand can't copy |
| **flavor** | 8 | The actual product, sensory, one flavor at a time |
| **seasonal** | 8 | Fall, Thanksgiving, Christmas, New Year, Easter, monsoon, back to school |
| **urgency** | 6 | Sunday 9pm, sold out, last two |
| **blessing** | 6 | The $9 blessing bag. Most shareable thing on the menu |
| **proof** | 6 | Reviews, messages, repeat rate. All real, all flagged |
| **origin** | 5 | Why she started, what it costs, why not in stores |
| **jar** | 5 | The returnable jar. Teaches the mechanic, does sustainability |
| **rewards / blend** | 4 | Buy 10 Get 1 Free, referrals, build-your-own jar |

### The weekly rhythm

Four posts a week, plus stories. This is deliberately sustainable — she is one
person with an oven. The rhythm mirrors her actual week, so the content is a
by-product of work she is already doing rather than a second job.

| Day | Post | Why then |
|---|---|---|
| **Sunday morning** | faith / useful | Quiet audience, high save rate |
| **Sunday 3–6pm** | **urgency** | The list closes at nine. This is the money post |
| **Monday** | **bake** | She is in the kitchen anyway. Film it while it happens |
| **Wed or Thu** | funny / flavor / local | Mid-week reach post |
| *Any day* | stories | Run-day porch shots, jars coming back, sold-out notices |

That is 4 a week = ~200 a year. The library of 100 covers **six months** with no
repeats, and the best twenty are worth reposting at six months anyway. Alongside
it she will be shooting real moments — those go in on top.

### How the library maps onto the calendar

Nine of the 100 carry a `season` tag (fall, Thanksgiving, Christmas, New Year,
Easter, monsoon, back to school). The rest are evergreen. The scheduler proposes
a month by taking the seasonal ones that fit the date first, then filling the rest
of the slots by rotating pillars so the same kind of post never lands twice in a
row.

### What each post contains

Every one of the 100 is not just a caption. It is:

- **hook** — the line that goes on the image, or the first line of the caption
- **caption** — finished, in her voice, ready to paste
- **cta** — one action, never five
- **shot** — plain-English direction on what to photograph or film, because this
  is the part that actually stops her: "Break one big cluster in half over the
  tray with your thumbs. Close, daylight, sound on. Cut it so it loops."
- **why** — one line on why the post works, so she learns the pattern instead of
  just consuming a list
- **when** — suggested day and time slot
- **hashtags** — five, local-weighted
- **needs** — present on 23 of them: the real fact she must supply first

## 4. Connecting Instagram and scheduling posts

### The honest summary

Publishing to Instagram from our own software is **possible, free, and does not
require Meta's app review** — as long as we are only ever posting to her own
account. That last clause is what makes this easy. If we were building a product
that posts on behalf of strangers, this would be a six-week compliance project.
We are not.

### What Meta requires

1. Her Instagram must be a **Professional account** (Business or Creator). Free,
   a settings toggle.
2. It must be **linked to a Facebook Page**. Free, five minutes. The Page can be
   empty and unused; it is a plumbing requirement, not a second account to run.
3. A **Meta developer app**, created once by us, left in **Development mode**.
4. Her Instagram account added to that app as an **Instagram Tester**, and the
   invite accepted from her Instagram settings.

Step 4 is the unlock. In Development mode, an app can publish to accounts that
have a role on it without ever going through App Review or business verification.
Since the only account we will ever post to is hers, we stay in Development mode
permanently.

Permissions requested: `instagram_business_basic` and
`instagram_business_content_publish`.

### How publishing actually works

Instagram's publishing API is a two-step handshake, not a single upload:

1. **Create a container** — `POST /<ig-user-id>/media` with a *public URL* to the
   image or video plus the caption. Instagram fetches the file from that URL
   itself.
2. **Poll the container** until its `status_code` is `FINISHED`. Images are near
   instant; video and reels take anywhere from a few seconds to a couple of
   minutes.
3. **Publish** — `POST /<ig-user-id>/media_publish` with the container id.

Two consequences shape the build:

- **The media has to be publicly hosted.** A photo on her phone cannot be posted
  directly. She uploads it to us, we store it at a public URL, and we hand that
  URL to Instagram. This means the build needs file storage — recommended:
  Vercel Blob, since the site is already a Next.js app.
- **Only JPEG for photos.** HEIC off an iPhone has to be converted on upload.
  This is a real papercut and it gets handled once, at upload, or every post
  fails confusingly.

Limits are generous: **100 API-published posts per 24 hours**, carousels counting
as one. She will post four a week.

### Scheduling: we build the clock, not Instagram

This is the most important architectural decision in the document.

Instagram's API does **not** reliably offer native future-scheduling the way
Facebook Pages do — it is undocumented for Instagram in Meta's own reference, and
it definitively does not exist for Stories or Reels. Every commercial scheduler —
Later, Planoly, Buffer — solves this the same way: **they hold the post in their
own database and call the publish API themselves at the right second.**

We do the same. It is more code than we would like and it is also more reliable,
because it means we control retries, we control token refresh, and a post that
fails tells her it failed instead of silently vanishing into Meta's queue.

**The shape of it:**

- A `social_posts` table holds every scheduled post: which library post, the
  media URL, the final caption, the scheduled time, and a status
  (`draft` → `scheduled` → `publishing` → `published` | `failed`).
- A **cron job runs every five minutes**, picks up anything due, creates the
  container, polls it, publishes it, and writes back the resulting Instagram
  media id.
- **Nothing is ever published twice.** Status transitions are guarded by a
  database lock, and the Instagram media id, once written, is the receipt. A
  crashed worker resumes rather than reposts.
- **Failures are loud.** If a post fails, it turns red on her page with a plain
  sentence saying what happened, and it does not silently retry forever. Three
  attempts, then it stops and tells her.

**Where the cron runs** is one decision for Brandon: Vercel Cron on a Pro plan
(five-minute granularity, ~$20/mo, zero extra infrastructure), or a free external
scheduler hitting a secret-protected route. Recommendation: Vercel Cron, because
the site is already there and one less moving part is worth $20.

### Tokens

Instagram long-lived tokens last **60 days** and can be refreshed any time after
day one. The system refreshes weekly, automatically. If a refresh ever fails —
she changes her password, revokes the app — the Social Media page shows a
**"Reconnect Instagram"** banner and scheduled posts pause rather than fail one
by one. This is the single most common way self-built posters break, so it is
designed for from the start rather than patched in later.

### What we are NOT going to do

- **Stories cannot be scheduled**, by anyone. If we want to post a story it has
  to happen at that moment. The page will treat stories as a checklist ("post a
  porch shot on the run today"), not as something it publishes.
- **No auto-generated captions at post time.** Everything is written in advance
  and reviewed by her. An AI writing in her voice unsupervised, on a faith brand,
  is a risk with no upside.
- **No auto-DM, no follow-bots, no engagement automation.** Against Meta's terms
  and against the brand.

### The alternative we considered and rejected

If the business already runs on a GoHighLevel subaccount, GHL's social planner
will connect Instagram in about ten minutes of clicking with no code at all. That
is a genuinely reasonable Plan B and worth doing on day one if she needs to be
posting *this week*.

It is not the recommendation, because the value of building it into her own
owner's page is that the scheduler can see the rest of the shop: it knows which
flavor just sold out, which verse is going out this week, whether the porch light
is off, and which day her next run is. A "Fill my month" button that pulls the
right seasonal posts and skips urgency posts on a week she is not baking is only
possible inside our own app. GHL cannot see any of that.

## 5. The page

A new **Social Media** tab in the owner's sidebar, between "Verse card" and
"Reviews". It has three screens, in the order she will use them.

### Screen 1 — The week

What she opens on a Sunday. The next seven days, four slots, each showing the
post that is queued for it: the hook, the picture, the time it goes out. Anything
missing a photo is obvious at a glance. One button at the top: **Fill my week**,
which picks four posts from the library that fit the season, skips anything she
has used recently, and drops them into the four slots.

Under it, a **shot list** — everything she needs to photograph this week, in one
list, so she can do it all in one morning in the kitchen instead of four times.

### Screen 2 — The library

All 100 posts. Filter by pillar, format, or goal. Search. Each card shows the
hook big, the caption underneath, and the shot direction in a tinted box, because
the shot direction is the part that actually gets her unstuck.

Every card has three actions: **Copy caption** (works today, no Instagram
connection needed), **Add a photo**, and **Schedule**. Posts that need a real
detail from her are marked with a small amber flag and cannot be scheduled until
she has filled it in — the system will not let her publish a caption with
`[first name]` still in it.

### Screen 3 — The calendar and the queue

A month view of what is scheduled, what has gone out, and what failed. Published
posts show their real numbers once the API reports them back. Failed posts show a
plain sentence and a Retry button.

Above it, the connection state: connected as `@herhandle`, token good for N days,
or a Reconnect banner if not.

### Design

It uses the design language the owner's side already has — cream, brown, sage,
the letterpress stamp buttons, Cormorant and Karla, the dashed hedge frames. It
should look like a page from her kitchen, not like a social media dashboard. No
charts, no engagement graphs, no vanity metrics. The only numbers on the page are
the ones that mean something.

## 6. Build plan

Six phases. **Phases 1 and 2 are useful on their own** — if we stop after them,
she has 100 posts and a shot list and she posts from her phone like everybody
else, and that is already most of the value.

| # | Phase | What she gets | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Library + tab** | The Social Media tab. All 100 posts, filters, search, copy-caption, mark-as-used. Works entirely without Instagram. | Nothing |
| **2** | **The week + shot list** | "Fill my week", the seven-day view, the one-morning shot list. | 1 |
| **3** | **Media** | Upload a photo or video from her phone against a post. HEIC converted, stored at a public URL. | 1 |
| **4** | **Connect Instagram** | Meta app, tester role, OAuth, token stored and auto-refreshed, Reconnect banner. | Nothing (parallel) |
| **5** | **The auto-poster** | Queue table, five-minute cron, container/poll/publish, retries, failure states, the calendar screen. | 3 + 4 |
| **6** | **Learn from it** *(optional)* | Read likes/saves/reach back per post, so the library can order itself by what actually works for her. | 5 |

**Lauren's side of it**, all in phase 4 and all one sitting:

1. Switch Instagram to a Professional account.
2. Link it to a Facebook Page (we can create the Page for her).
3. Accept an Instagram Tester invite.
4. Sign in once through the site.

### Decisions needed from Brandon

1. **Cron host** — Vercel Pro (~$20/mo, recommended) or a free external cron.
2. **Storage** — Vercel Blob (recommended) or Cloudflare R2.
3. **Stop after phase 2?** Library + shot list only, no Instagram connection.
   Cheapest path to most of the value; entirely defensible.
4. **GHL as an interim** — if she needs to be posting this week, connect
   Instagram in GoHighLevel now and migrate to this later.

### Risks, honestly

- **The Facebook Page requirement** confuses people and is where this stalls. It
  is five minutes with someone sitting next to her, and hours alone.
- **Token expiry** breaks self-built posters more than anything else. Designed
  for: weekly auto-refresh, loud banner, posts pause instead of failing.
- **HEIC photos** will silently break publishing if not converted at upload.
- **Meta changes the API.** It happens. The queue is ours, so a breaking change
  means posts sit unpublished with a clear error rather than disappearing.
- **The real risk is not technical.** It is that she does not have the photos.
  That is why the shot list exists in phase 2, before any of the API work.

## 7. Technical appendix

**Files this touches**

```
site/content/social-posts.json      the 100 posts (built, in the repo now)
site/lib/social.ts                  types, library loader, the week-filler
site/lib/instagram.ts               Graph API client: container, poll, publish
site/lib/instagram-auth.ts          OAuth, long-lived token store, refresh
site/lib/social-queue.ts            queue reads/writes, status machine
site/app/owner/social/page.tsx      the tab (three screens)
site/components/owner/social/*      Week, Library, Calendar, PostCard, Connect
site/app/api/social/publish/route.ts    the cron target, secret-protected
site/app/api/social/connect/route.ts    OAuth callback
site/scripts/social.test.mjs        tests for the filler and the status machine
```

**Database**

```sql
create table social_queue (
  id            bigserial primary key,
  library_id    text,                    -- 'hh-042', null for one-offs
  caption       text not null,
  media_url     text,                    -- public JPEG/MP4 URL
  media_type    text not null,           -- IMAGE | VIDEO | REELS | CAROUSEL
  scheduled_at  timestamptz not null,
  status        text not null default 'draft',
  container_id  text,
  ig_media_id   text,                    -- the receipt. Never publish twice.
  attempts      int  not null default 0,
  last_error    text,
  created_at    timestamptz not null default now(),
  updated_at    timestamptz not null default now()
);
create index on social_queue (status, scheduled_at);

-- the connection lives in the existing settings table, key 'instagram'
```

**The publish worker, in order**

1. `select ... for update skip locked` anything `scheduled` and due. This is what
   makes a double-run harmless.
2. Mark `publishing`, increment `attempts`.
3. `POST /<ig-id>/media` → `container_id`.
4. Poll `GET /<container_id>?fields=status_code` until `FINISHED`, up to 60s for
   images and 5 min for video.
5. `POST /<ig-id>/media_publish` → `ig_media_id`, mark `published`.
6. On error: write `last_error`, back to `scheduled` if `attempts < 3`, else
   `failed`.

**Rate limit** — check `GET /<ig-id>/content_publishing_limit` before a run.
100/24h; we will use 4/week.
